A small machine is one you can hold in your head. Its parts are few enough to name, and when it breaks you can say where. Most of what surrounds us is not like this: it is too large to see whole, and we meet it only through its surfaces, which are designed to be pleasant and to explain nothing.

The argument for small machines is not nostalgia. It is an argument about attention. A thing you understand asks less of you than a thing you merely trust, and the attention it frees can go elsewhere, toward people, or weather, or the slow work of reading.
